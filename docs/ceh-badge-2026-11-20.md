# Certified Ethical Hacker badge on the About page (20 November 2026)

Status: draft. Implemented at the owner's request (Alex Gusevs) on 20 November 2026, not yet reviewed. The reading of the EC-Council rules below is ours and is not confirmed with EC-Council.

## What changed

The EC-Council Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) badge is shown on the About page, in the "Quality and information security" block at `/team/#certification`, in its own row under the ISO statement. The footer is unchanged.

Text beside the badge (`ethicalHackerCertification` in `app/components/EthicalHackerBadge.tsx`):

> A member of our team holds the Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) certification from EC-Council.
>
> Certified Ethical Hacker is the registered trademark of EC-Council.

Owner decisions of 20 November 2026: the holder is not named on the site, and the badge appears on the About page only, not in the footer. A second sentence in the first draft ("The certification is personal to that team member.") was removed at the owner's request the same day.

Artwork: `public/images/certification/ec-council-ceh.png`. It is the badge file the owner supplied (1501 x 1501 px), scaled down in proportion to 420 x 420 px and otherwise unchanged. The supplied file name carried a code that may identify the individual credential. It was not kept, because this repository is public and the holder is not named.

## Why the wording is about a person

CEH is awarded to individuals. Unlike ISO 9001 and ISO/IEC 27001 it does not certify the company, so the site must not say or imply that Double Helix Technologies is "CEH certified". That is why the badge has its own row and its own sentence with "a member of our team" as the subject, and why it is not placed beside the ISO mark in the footer.

## How the EC-Council rules are applied

Sources: "EC-Council Logo Usage Guidelines" v3.0 (cert.eccouncil.org) and the "EC-Council Trademark and Brand Guideline" (eccouncil.org, file dated December 2025). Both were read on 20 November 2026 through an automated text extraction, not page by page. Check each point against the documents before relying on it.

| Rule as we read it | How the site meets it |
| --- | --- |
| Only for a certification that is held and current, not after it lapses or is suspended or revoked | Owner's statement that a team member holds it. Not checked against the certificate (open point 3). |
| Used only to identify the holder, with no suggestion of endorsement by or affiliation with EC-Council | The sentence says that a member of the team holds the certification, not the company. |
| Artwork unchanged, each logo on its own | Shown as supplied, resized only, not combined with the ISO mark. |
| Certification logo at least 80 px tall on screen | 84 x 84 px box. The badge itself is about 83 px tall inside the file's transparent margins. |
| Not larger or more prominent than our own name and logo | The 84 x 84 px box (7,056 square px) is smaller in area than the 180 x 44 px header logo (7,920 square px). It is taller than the logo, so this relies on reading "larger" as overall size, the same reading used for the ISO mark. |
| Clear space around it (0.3 cm recommended) | 24 px to the text beside it, 28 px or more to the ISO mark above. |
| Trademark ownership legend where practical | The form the brand guideline gives as preferred, in small print under the sentence. |

## Open points

1. Employer use. Both documents are written for the certified person's own use (business cards, their own website, marketing of their own services). Neither says whether an employer may show an employee's badge on the company website. Ask EC-Council in writing, or have the holder ask, and keep the answer with this note. Until then this is an unconfirmed interpretation: record the risk and who accepted it.
2. Holder not named. The guidelines allow the logo to identify the holder. A badge without a named holder is a weaker fit with that purpose than naming the person, which would need the holder's agreement.
3. Holder's agreement and validity. Record outside this public repository who holds the certification, the certificate number and the expiry date, and that the holder agrees to this use. Remove the badge when the certification lapses or the person leaves the company.
4. Version number. The logo guidelines ask for the certification version next to the logo, except for the ANSI-accredited CEH, which carries none. Confirm which applies to this credential.
5. Dark theme. The same full-colour badge is shown on the dark theme. The brand guideline's rules on dark backgrounds could not be read reliably from the text extraction. Check its pages on logo do's and don'ts.
6. Not done: the badge and its sentence are not in `/llms.txt`, the structured data, the footer or the homepage. Separate decisions.
