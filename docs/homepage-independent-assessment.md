# Independent assessment of the reworked homepage

Date: 14 September 2026, evening
Subject: static export of branch `feat/homepage-decision-maker` at commit 9257262, served locally; not deployed
Reviewer: Claude (Cowork), as a reviewer separate from the implementing session, at Alex's request
Status: draft for internal review

Note from the implementing session: this assessment was produced by a fresh reviewer that saw only the export, the component sources and two context documents. Three of its findings were then checked independently against the export and hold: the server-rendered HTML carries the dark theme's logo (`logo-full-light.svg`) and a "Switch to light mode" toggle while the document has no `dark` class; the "Customers and partners" band is empty without JavaScript; and the homepage mixes British and American spelling (one "organisation", six "organization"). The reduced-motion hydration error (React 418) is consistent with how `Hero.tsx` renders the sparkles wrapper on the server and omits it on a reduced-motion client. The findings about Mainos, Krafthub, Veractis.io and helixtech.bio rest on the reviewer's web checks and should be read against what the owner knows about those relationships.

## The question

The reader this page must convince is a managing director, operations lead or procurement lead at a genomics service laboratory, a diagnostics company or a forensic laboratory. They will give the page a few minutes and the claims perhaps ten minutes of checking: the company register, the named partners, the legal pages. Two things matter: can they judge relevance and credibility in one visit, and do the claims survive that checking. Attractiveness matters only where it helps or hinders those judgements.

## What I checked

I rendered the export with Chromium through Playwright at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, light and dark, once without JavaScript and once with reduced motion, dismissed the cookie banner where present, and looked at every screenshot; fonts were system fallbacks, so I say nothing about typefaces. I read the server-rendered HTML for heading order, landmarks, ARIA, link targets and final figure values, scanned all 39 exported pages for banned characters and abbreviations, drove both carousels by keyboard, timed the auto-advance with and without reduced motion, and captured the page before hydration. I read the About, Our work, legal notice, privacy and three case pages. Externally I looked up veractis.io, helixtech.bio, krafthub.ai, mainos.lv, the Lursoft entry, the business.gov.lv profile and the cal.com page, booking nothing and treating everything found as data. I read the implementer's record and the morning review only after forming my own view.

## First impression, before checking anything

The first screen does its job. At 1440 the headline, one sentence and two buttons sit on a soft mesh with a band of small sparkles, and the logo marquee is pinned to the foot of the screen. At 390 the headline wraps to four lines ("Higher / throughput. / Lower running / costs."), heavy but legible, and the primary button sits above the cookie banner. The dark theme works. The decoration is restrained and never competes with the text; I would keep it.

"Higher throughput. Lower running costs." is understandable alone, and "throughput" is a word a laboratory director uses daily. It is also broad enough to head a logistics firm or a cloud host; the sentence beneath supplies what, for whom and where. My reservation is the second half: a laboratory reader hears "the laboratory's running costs", while the only evidence is a 37% cut in one client's annual IT costs. Supportable, but only read more narrowly than most visitors will read it.

Scrolling on, the order is right for a buyer: proof, then the six kinds of work, then who is accountable, then how delivery works, then quotes, then one invitation to talk. The services are concrete and readable without clicking; the team block gives four faces, roles and full biographies, long on a phone but the right content.

## What the checks found

Concentration of evidence. Four of seven cases and all four quotes are Eurofins Genomics, and the quote carousel repeats the quotes after they have appeared on the case slides, so the visitor reads each twice. The four outcomes the hero rests on (75% less order setup time, delivery from 1.5 days to 3 hours, onboarding in under two weeks, IT costs down 37%) are all Eurofins Genomics cases. Of the other three, the forensic case is anonymous but on sector; the observability case (support teams in four global regions) and the MVP case (a field scheduling process) are anonymous and not identified as life sciences at all. The named, on-sector evidence on this page comes from one client group. That is honest, but a procurement lead will ask for a second reference before signing.

The marquee. Three customer logos show: Eurofins Genomics, Mainos and Krafthub. I verified that mainos.lv is a Latvian second-hand marketplace and that krafthub.ai is Krafthub GmbH, near Munich, founded in 2025, selling AI quoting software to construction trades. Neither is in life sciences or has a case on the site. Three of the five partner entries are membership associations. Veractis.io exists and is relevant, a white-label platform for test-kit registration, consent and result delivery for laboratories. The fifth is typeset "helix.tech.bio", but its domain is helixtech.bio and its own pages write HelixTech; it is a software consultancy for biotech startups. A visitor typing the name as shown reaches nothing, and "helix.tech" beside "Double Helix Technologies" invites the question whether it is a sister company. The record says Krafthub, Veractis.io and helixtech.bio share leadership, which I could not confirm publicly; if true, three of eight logos in a band labelled "Customers and partners" are one related group. Neither partner has yet agreed to appear.

Company facts. The registered office on the About page and in the legal notice (Lastādijas iela 12 k-3, Riga, LV-1050) matches the Lursoft entry exactly, which also gives 8 October 2021 as the registration date. The business.gov.lv English profile still shows the implausible turnover flagged this morning, lists eight Western European export markets with targets across the EEA, and does not mention North America. The homepage says "Europe and North America" and the structured data names the United States as an area served; the only public signals I found are the Latvian American Chamber of Commerce membership and one anonymous case with support teams partly in the USA. The claim may be true; nothing a buyer can find supports it.

Static HTML and JavaScript. Every figure on all seven slides is a final value in the HTML. The ISO statement appears only on the About page in the approved wording and the structured data says "certification in progress", but the keywords meta tag still says "ISO 9001 and ISO 27001 certification". Without JavaScript the page is complete except that the "Customers and partners" label sits over an empty band, because the marquee renders only on the client, and the header logo is the white variant on a white background, invisible, with the theme toggle in its dark-mode state. Every visitor sees the same before hydration: a flicker on a fast connection, seconds of invisible logo on a slow one. Under reduced motion the carousels stay still, the marquee is paused, the mesh does not drift and no sparkle canvas exists, exactly right, but the console logs React error 418, a hydration mismatch; the error is verified, and from the Hero source I infer the cause is that the sparkles wrapper is rendered on the server and omitted on a reduced-motion client.

Accessibility. Landmarks and section labels are present. The "How we work" steps are h3 headings whose text is only the digit. The six off-screen slide headings are not hidden or inert, so a screen reader hears all seven and a keyboard user tabs through all seven case links while the carousel flips past; tolerable. Worse, the marquee renders its eight logos twice for the loop without hiding the clones, so the keyboard user meets sixteen tab stops of moving targets before the first case. The carousel controls work by keyboard (arrow keys inside a slide, Enter on arrows and dots), but the dots are 8 pixels tall and 8 or 16 wide, far under the 24 by 24 pixel target WCAG 2.2 asks for, and on a phone the quote carousel has no arrow buttons, leaving 320 pixels of empty space under the shorter quotes (180 on desktop). The footer's social icons are links nested inside buttons, which is invalid HTML.

Copy. No em dashes, middle dots or unit abbreviations anywhere in the export. The homepage mixes spellings within a screen: "organisation", "standardised" and "programme" beside "organization" six times, "prioritized", "optimization" and "centers". The export agreement number is set with non-breaking hyphens, so anyone who copies it gets non-standard characters, and "Financed by European Union" should be checked against the wording the LIAA agreement requires. The privacy policy is dated "11/02/2026", ambiguous where the legal notice uses 2026-09-14; it names no controller address, gives no retention periods and does not mention that booking a call sends the visitor's details to cal.com. Whether that meets GDPR Articles 13 and 14 is for the owner of the legal content to check; a procurement reviewer will read it, and it is thin for a company offering "Security, Compliance & AI Governance Readiness".

Consistency and links. The homepage says "Based in Riga"; the About page says "Based in Europe". "A team of around 20" appears only on the homepage; the About page shows the same four leaders with an external LinkedIn link as the only route to the rest, so "Meet the rest of the team" leads to the same four people. The founding year is stated nowhere and the homepage title is the only one without the company name. All 23 internal links resolve; the external ones are LinkedIn, two mailboxes and the cal.com page, which opens for the named host but is client-rendered, so I could not confirm "30 to 60 minutes". Both calls to action and the only visible email route to one person's calendar and mailbox; the general mailbox is a footer icon only, and a procurement team has no route for a request for information.

## Defects a buyer would actually hit

A genomics buyer clicks Krafthub or Mainos and finds a construction startup and a second-hand marketplace with no case study, which undoes the specialist positioning the rest of the page builds. The evidence is one named client group behind the hero figures, four of seven cases and all four quotes, shown twice. The logo is invisible and the logo band empty on first paint, permanently without JavaScript. "Meet the rest of the team" is a dead end. Contact means one person's calendar and mailbox. The privacy policy is thin and nothing covers data handling, security testing, access to customer environments, intellectual property, continuity or insurance, the questions that decide whether a forensic or diagnostics buyer sends the supplier questionnaire. On a phone the quote carousel leaves about 320 pixels empty under the shorter quotes, with 8 pixel dots as the only controls.

## What changed since the morning review

Most of the morning's concrete defects are fixed, and I confirmed each: one headline and two actions; /about/ redirects to /team/ with noindex and a canonical link and is out of the sitemap; the counters are gone; the legal notice carries legal form, registration number, register, postal code and VAT number; one address, matching the register; services readable without clicking; Riga and the team size stated; client logos shown with permission recorded; no AI in the headline; ISO wording confined to the approved statement. The page is better organised than the live one.

What has not changed is the shape of the evidence; the single-client concentration is more visible now that quotes appear twice and every hero outcome comes from that client. Contact still goes to one person, the security page still does not exist, two memberships remain unconfirmed on the associations' own sites and the business.gov.lv turnover is still wrong. New in this build, introduced by the rework, are the off-sector customer logos, the partner wordmark that does not match its domain, the sixteen marquee tab stops and the reduced-motion hydration error. Whether the pre-hydration logo problem predates the rework, the export cannot tell me.

## Verdict

As the buyer I would book the call. The page says in one screen what the company does and for whom, gives numbers I can trace to written cases, shows who is accountable and does not overclaim on certification. As the procurement lead I would not yet put a six-figure engagement through on the strength of the site: one client group, nothing on data handling or security, a thin privacy policy, a founder's calendar as the only door. None of that is a design problem and the design should not be reworked again; the remaining gap is editorial and factual, about a day of work plus two owner decisions.

## Prioritised recommendations

First, the marquee: show only customers that are on sector and have a case on the site, or split the band into "Selected customers" and "Memberships and partners"; correct the helixtech.bio wordmark; hold both partner logos until the organisations have agreed and supplied artwork; and if the shared-leadership claim is true, decide whether to say so or leave those logos out. Second, evidence breadth: drop the quotes from the case slides so each appears once, and pursue one named reference outside Eurofins Genomics, even one attributed sentence from the forensic customer. Third, render the light theme's logo and toggle by default or from the stored preference, and mount the sparkles only on the client so reduced-motion visitors get no hydration error. Fourth, list the wider team on /team/ or relabel the link "About us" and move "around 20" there beside the founding year. Fifth, put hello@doublehelix.dev as visible text in the contact section with one sentence for procurement and supplier questionnaires. Sixth, legal tidy: controller identity and address, retention, the cal.com and Google Meet processors, an unambiguous date, standard hyphens in the agreement number, the keywords tag aligned to the ISO wording. Seventh, fit the quote carousel to the active slide as the case carousel does, add arrows on phones and enlarge the dots to at least 24 pixels. Eighth, copy: one spelling convention, rewrite "-80%+" (which reads as minus eighty per cent plus) and the "1st" statistic, put the step names inside the h3 headings, hide the marquee clones from assistive technology, fix the link-in-button icons. Ninth, larger: the security and data-handling page already listed as owner item 7; until it exists the compliance-readiness service sells something the company does not demonstrate about itself. Tenth, confirm North America with something citable or soften the claim.

## Decisions worth recording

The criterion for which logos appear (on sector, with a published case, with written permission) and whether related-party partners appear at all belongs in an ADR or the project log; it recurs with every new client or partner. The geographic claim should be recorded with the evidence the company would cite if challenged. The contact routing choice (founder's calendar only, or a general mailbox alongside) is a business decision with a credibility cost and should be recorded as such. The decision to keep two auto-advancing carousels should be recorded with the reduced-motion and keyboard behaviour verified here, and the server-rendered theme default deserves a short ADR. The open owner items in the implementer's record (board members, data-protection contact, Eurofins titles, assessment pricing, the security page) belong in the issue tracker with owners and dates, not in code comments.
